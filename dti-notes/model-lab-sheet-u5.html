🧪 Activity 5.1Unit V

Classify Existing Products as Incremental / Disruptive / Radical

⏱ 30 min👥 Individual

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Pick 4 real products (local or known brands).
  2. Classify each: Incremental / Disruptive / Radical.
  3. Also tag: Product / Process / Service / Social.
  4. Write one “why” sentence per item — no empty boxes.

📊 Classification Table — Model Answer

1 UPI / PhonePe payments
Disruptive · Service
Why: Replaced cash and card habits for millions with a simpler, cheaper channel.
2 New smartphone camera megapixels
Incremental · Product
Why: Improves an existing product line without changing the market structure.
3 mRNA COVID vaccines
Radical · Product / Process
Why: New science platform that changed how vaccines are designed and produced.
4 BusPing (our concept)
Disruptive · Service
Why: Serves students poorly served by inaccurate apps, using SMS they already have — simpler, lower barrier.

🧠 Reflection

1. Surprise: Many “new” phone features are incremental, not radical — novelty ≠ radical innovation.

2. Disruptive + radical? Rare but possible: a radical tech that also undercuts incumbents on simplicity/price (e.g. early PCs). BusPing is disruptive/service, not radical science.

🧪 Activity 5.2Unit V

Identify Three Innovation Opportunities from Campus/Community

⏱ 45 min👥 GroupDomain: Campus transport & student life

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Observe campus/community pain points.
  2. Write 3 opportunities with Need, Opportunity, Who/Pain/Why now.
  3. Avoid vague problems; include timing (“why now”).

💡 Three Innovation Opportunities — Model

1 Need: Trustworthy bus ETA · Opportunity: BusPing SMS service
Who: College students · Pain: Missed classes · Why now: Apps often wrong; SMS still universal on campus.
2 Need: Fair hostel mess feedback · Opportunity: Anonymous daily rating + menu tip
Who: Hostel residents · Pain: Complaints ignored · Why now: Digital forms + admin dashboards are cheap.
3 Need: Safe late-night campus walk · Opportunity: Peer “walk buddy” match by route/time
Who: Students after lab/library · Pain: Alone on dark paths · Why now: Phones + student ID auth make matching easy.

🧠 Reflection

1. Most feasible in 4 weeks: BusPing — already prototyped in Unit IV; can run a campus pilot with a shared phone number.

2. Scales beyond campus: BusPing pattern (simple ETA by code) can extend to other city routes and colleges.

🧪 Activity 5.3Unit V

Prepare a Value Proposition Canvas

⏱ 50 min👥 Individual / GroupSolution: BusPing

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Fill Customer Profile: Jobs, Pains, Gains (from persona/interviews).
  2. Fill Value Map: Products/Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators.
  3. Write one-sentence value proposition.
  4. Check fit strength; note weak pairs.

👤 Customer Profile + 📦 Value Map — Model

Jobs
Reach college on time; plan morning without stress; use a phone they already have.
Products / Services
SMS/USSD short-code ETA; optional leave-by time; save-as-contact prompt.
Pains
Wrong app times; missed classes; anxiety waiting; no data on basic phones.
Pain Relievers
Real ETA in one message; works offline-ish (SMS); no app install.
Gains
Confidence to leave later; fewer late marks; simple daily habit.
Gain Creators
“Leave by HH:MM”; reliable enough to plan around; peer word-of-mouth.

💎 Value Proposition (one sentence)

BusPing gives college students a trustworthy bus arrival time in one SMS — so they leave on time without relying on broken apps.

🧠 Reflection

1. Strongest fit: Pain (wrong times / no data) ↔ Reliever (SMS ETA on any phone).

2. Weaker fit: Gain “peer status” is soft; focus remains on reliability and leave-by support.

🧪 Activity 5.4Unit V

Validate the Proposed Innovation with Potential Users

⏱ 60 min👥 GroupSolution: BusPing

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Talk to ~5 target users (not only friends).
  2. Ask: would use? how often? would pay? amount?
  3. Capture one key quote.
  4. Avoid leading questions.

📊 Validation Summary — Model (n≈5)

Would use?Yes 5 / No 0
How often?Most: every morning on class days; some also evening return
Would pay?Yes 3 / No 2 · Amount: ₹0–20/month (prefer free or college-bundled)
Key quote“If it works when the app lies, I’ll use it every day — just don’t make me download anything.”

🧠 Reflection

1. Willingness-to-pay: Lower than hoped — students expect free campus services; partner with college or ads later.

2. Change before next test: Pilot as free college service first; test paid only after habit forms.

🧪 Activity 5.5Unit V

Prepare an Introductory Business Model Canvas

⏱ 50 min👥 GroupVenture: BusPing

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Fill all 9 BMC blocks using VPC + validation.
  2. Avoid “everyone” as segment.
  3. Include a rough break-even note.

📋 BMC — 9 Blocks (Model)

Customer SegmentsCollege students (daily bus riders); secondary: parents, other city colleges
Value PropositionsTrustworthy ETA by SMS; leave-by time; no app needed
ChannelsCampus posters, hostel WhatsApp, student union, short-code publicity
Customer RelationshipsSelf-serve SMS; light support via college helpdesk
Revenue StreamsCollege licence / pilot fee; later optional premium alerts; possible operator partnership
Key ResourcesShort code / number; ETA data feed or manual ops; simple backend
Key ActivitiesKeep ETAs accurate; onboard colleges; support codes; iterate replies
Key PartnershipsCollege admin; bus operators / GPS vendors; SMS gateway
Cost StructureSMS gateway, number rental, student ops time, basic hosting

💡 Rough Break-even

If one college pays ₹15,000–25,000 per semester for a pilot, gateway + ops costs can be covered for a small multi-route service; free student use, college as payer.

🧠 Reflection

1. Hardest block: Revenue — students resist paying; college-as-customer is more realistic.

2. Dependency: Yes — needs operator data or reliable manual/crowdsourced updates we don’t fully control.

🧪 Activity 5.6Unit V

Identify Possible IPR Protection

⏱ 30 min👥 IndividualProject: BusPing

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. List protectable elements (name, logo, code, unique method).
  2. Map to Patent / Trademark / Copyright / Design.
  3. Note what cannot be protected (bare ideas, public facts).

©️ IPR Mapping — Model

1Element: Brand name “BusPing” · Type: Trademark · Register name + logo early
2Element: App/backend source code · Type: Copyright · Automatic on creation; keep repos private
3Element: Distinctive logo / icon · Type: Trademark + Design (if unique visual)
4Element: Specific technical method (if novel) · Type: Patent only if inventive step + documentation — unlikely for simple SMS wrapper

⚠️ Cannot be protected

The general idea “send a code, get bus time”; public timetable data; generic SMS patterns already in use.

🧠 Reflection

1. File first: Trademark for BusPing — cheap, protects brand as we pitch publicly.

2. Risk of no protection: Another group copies the name; confusion on campus and in contests.

🧪 Activity 5.7Unit V

Prepare and Deliver a 3-Minute Innovation Pitch

⏱ 90 min👥 Group

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Write 6 beats × ~30 seconds each.
  2. Rehearse with timer; cut text-heavy slides.
  3. End with a clear ask (pilot, feedback, funding, partnership).

🎤 Six Beats Script — Model

Problem (30s): Every morning, students like Ravi check a bus app that is often wrong. They leave early or arrive late — missed classes and constant stress.
Solution (30s): BusPing: text a short code, get a real ETA by SMS. No app, works on basic phones. Optional “leave by” time so you know when to walk out the door.
Market (30s): One mid-size college has thousands of daily bus riders. The same pattern fits other colleges and city routes where apps fail and SMS still works.
Model (30s): Free for students; college pays a semester pilot fee. Later: operator partnerships and optional premium alerts. Low SMS gateway cost.
Team (30s): Design Thinking team that already observed stops, interviewed riders, prototyped, and tested with real students — we know the friction points.
Ask (30s): Approve a 4-week free pilot on two campus routes, with one shared number and student ops support. We will report on-time improvement and usage.

🧠 Reflection

1. Hardest to compress: Market — easy to over-explain; keep to “thousands of daily riders, same pain elsewhere.”

2. Feedback to act on: Lead with the student story, not the tech; make the ask specific and time-bound.
