🧪 Activity 2.1 Unit II

Affinity Mapping of Interview Data

⏱ 45 min 👥 Group 📘 § 2.1 Topic: College student bus commute

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Bring interview notes and observations from Unit I.
  2. Write each fact / quote on a separate sticky note.
  3. Group similar notes into clusters on a wall or board.
  4. Name each cluster with a clear theme (not vague labels).
  5. Write one insight (the “why”) under every theme.
  6. Answer the reflection questions as a group.
📐 Diagram — Affinity Mapping Flow
U1 Interview notes + Observations
↓ group into themes
1. Unreliable timing
Missed classes
2. No seating
Fatigue & discomfort
3. Unclear announcements
Miss correct bus
4. Broken queues
Chaos & stress
↓ each theme needs an insight (why)

🧩 Theme Clusters — Model Answer (Bus commute)

1. Theme Unreliable timing information
Insight (why): Students miss classes because apps and boards do not match real bus arrivals.
2. Theme Lack of seating at stops
Insight (why): Long standing causes fatigue and discomfort, especially for elderly and tired students.
3. Theme Unclear announcements
Insight (why): People cannot hear or understand route/stop calls, so they ask strangers or miss the right bus.
4. Theme Informal, broken queues
Insight (why): Without clear boarding rules, chaos increases stress and delays when the bus arrives.

🧠 Reflection — Model Answer

1. Theme that surprised the group most: How often “unreliable timing” appeared across almost every interview and observation.

2. Insight that could become a design opportunity: Reliable real-time bus tracking + clearer stop information could reduce missed classes and anxiety.

🧪 Activity 2.2 Unit II

Develop Three Alternative Problem Statements

⏱ 40 min 👥 Individual 📘 § 2.2 Theme: Unreliable bus timing information

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Pick one strong theme from the affinity map (2.1).
  2. Write three different problem statements using: [User] needs [need] because [insight].
  3. Change the framing each time (different user focus, different need, or different insight).
  4. Avoid vague users like “people” or “everyone”.
  5. Always include the insight (the “because”).
📐 Diagram — Three Framings of the Same Theme
Theme: Unreliable bus timing
↓ frame three ways
1. Student focus — accurate real-time info to avoid missing class
2. Commuter focus — dependable wait estimates to reduce anxiety
3. New rider focus — clear confirmation without asking strangers

📝 Three Statements — Model Answer

1. College students need accurate, real-time bus arrival information because current apps and boards are often wrong and cause them to miss classes.
2. Daily bus commuters need dependable wait-time estimates at the stop because uncertainty creates anxiety and forces them to leave home much earlier than necessary.
3. First-time and infrequent riders need clear route and timing confirmation at the stop because they currently depend on asking strangers and still miss the correct bus.

🧠 Reflection — Model Answer

1. Hardest statement: Statement 3 — framing for infrequent riders needed a different insight than the student-focused ones.

2. How framing changes solutions: Statement 1 points toward better apps/boards; statement 2 toward stress-reducing wait tools; statement 3 toward simpler on-stop guidance for newcomers.

🧪 Activity 2.3 Unit II

Prepare POV and HMW Statements

⏱ 45 min 👥 Individual 📘 § 2.3 Problem: Student needs accurate real-time bus info

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Choose one problem statement from 2.2.
  2. Rewrite it as a POV: “User needs X because Y.”
  3. Generate at least 5 “How Might We…?” questions from that POV.
  4. Keep HMWs open (not yes/no, not “build an app”).
  5. Pick which HMW opens the most creative space.
📐 Diagram — POV → HMW Bridge
POV: Ravi needs trustworthy real-time arrival info because wrong timings make him late and stressed
↓ How Might We…?
HMW know time before leaving home? HMW clear info at the stop? HMW reduce wait anxiety? HMW help infrequent riders? HMW make info plan-worthy?

💬 POV Statement — Model Answer

POV: Ravi, a college student who depends on the bus every morning, needs trustworthy real-time arrival information because wrong timings make him late to class and increase daily stress.

❓ HMW Questions — Model Answer

  1. How might we help students know the true arrival time of their bus before they leave home?
  2. How might we make wait-time information clear and visible at the bus stop itself?
  3. How might we reduce the anxiety of “Will the bus come on time?” during the morning commute?
  4. How might we help infrequent riders confirm the correct bus without asking strangers?
  5. How might we turn unreliable public transport info into something students can plan their day around?

🧠 Reflection — Model Answer

1. HMW that opened the most creative space: #3 — focusing on anxiety allows solutions beyond apps (seating, community updates, status lights, etc.).

2. HMW to start ideating on: #1 — real-time info before leaving home has high impact on punctuality.

🧪 Activity 2.4 Unit II

Stakeholder Map

⏱ 40 min 👥 Individual / Group 📘 § 2.4 Project: Reliable bus info for student commuters

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. List everyone affected by or affecting the problem (not only the end user).
  2. Place each stakeholder in one of four boxes: Manage Closely, Keep Satisfied, Keep Informed, Monitor.
  3. Use influence (power to change things) and interest (how much they care).
  4. Note anyone you almost forgot.
  5. Identify who could block or boost a solution.

high influence, high interest		 College students (daily riders) · Transport authority / bus operators · College admin (attendance & punctuality)

📢 Keep Informed

🧪 Activity 2.5 Unit II

Root-Cause Analysis (5 Whys)

⏱ 40 min 👥 Individual / Group 📘 § 2.4 Problem: Students miss class due to wrong bus timings

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Write the visible problem clearly.
  2. Ask “Why?” and answer using evidence from research (not pure guesses).
  3. Repeat “Why?” on the previous answer until you reach a root cause (usually 5 levels).
  4. Do not change the question form — always “Why?”
  5. Propose one countermeasure that attacks the root, not only the symptom.
📐 Diagram — 5 Whys Chain
P: Students late — bus time wrong on app/board
↓ Why?
Displayed time ≠ actual location
↓ Why?
Data updated rarely / not live
↓ Why?
No GPS feed linked to public boards
↓ Why?
System built for fixed timetables only
↓ Why?
🎯 Root: No shared real-time tracking passengers can trust

🌳 The Why Chain — Model Answer

P · Problem Students often reach college late because the bus does not arrive when the app/board shows.
1 · Why? → The displayed arrival time does not match the actual bus location.
2 · Why? → Location/schedule data is updated rarely or not at all in real time.
3 · Why? → Many buses have no live GPS feed, or the feed is not connected to public boards/apps.
4 · Why? → Transport systems were designed around fixed timetables, not live tracking.
5 · Why? → Root → Root cause: There is no reliable, shared real-time tracking system that passengers and stop displays can trust.

💡 Proposed Countermeasure

Introduce (or improve) a live bus-tracking feed that updates both mobile apps and stop boards every few minutes, so displayed times match reality.

🧠 Reflection — Model Answer

1. Did the root surprise you? Partly — many people blame “the driver” or “traffic,” but the deeper issue is missing live data infrastructure.

2. Why fixing only symptoms is temporary: Adding more seats or louder announcements does not stop wrong timings; only fixing the information system reduces late arrivals at the source.

🧪 Activity 2.6 Unit II

Select and Validate One Problem / Opportunity

⏱ 50 min 👥 Group 📘 § 2.5 · 2.6 · 2.8

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. List 3–4 candidate problems from earlier work.
  2. Score each on Impact and Feasibility (e.g. 1–5).
  3. Talk to 3 real users (with consent) to check if the top problem is real for them.
  4. Record yes/no and short notes for each user.
  5. Lock one final problem only after validation.

📊 Candidate Scoring — Model Answer

A Unreliable real-time bus timing info — Impact: 5 · Feasibility: 4
B No seating at bus stops — Impact: 4 · Feasibility: 3
C Unclear verbal announcements — Impact: 3 · Feasibility: 4
D Chaotic boarding queues — Impact: 3 · Feasibility: 3

✅ Validation Results — Model Answer

User 1 Yes — “I leave 20 minutes early every day because the app is wrong.”
User 2 Yes — “Last week I missed two classes in a row waiting for a bus that never came on time.”
User 3 Yes — “Boards at the stop show old times; I always ask other people.”

🎯 Final Selected Problem

Selected: College students need accurate, real-time bus arrival information because current apps and stop boards are often wrong, causing missed classes and daily stress.

🧠 Reflection — Model Answer

1. Did validation change the choice? No — all three users confirmed the timing problem; it stayed the top candidate.

2. Evidence that the problem is real: Unit I observations + interviews + three validation conversations all point to the same issue (wrong / outdated timing info).
