🧪 Activity 1.1 Unit I

Observation of a Real-Life Problem

🎯 Objective: Observe users in a natural setting 📍 Scenario: Bus stop waiting area

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Choose a public place (bus stop, canteen, library, etc.).
  2. Sit quietly — do not talk to people or interrupt them.
  3. Watch for at least 20–30 minutes.
  4. Write only facts you see (not opinions or guesses).
  5. List exactly 5 raw observations in the blank sheet.
  6. Answer the 3 reflection questions honestly.

👀 Example A — Bus Stop (Model Answer)

  1. People repeatedly check bus arrival boards.
  2. A student uses a mobile app but complains about inaccurate timings.
  3. Elderly passengers stand uncomfortably due to lack of seating.
  4. Commuters form informal queues but break them when buses arrive.
  5. Several passengers ask others for bus route confirmation.

Surprised most by: Frequent frustration with bus timing information.

Hidden need: Reliable real-time bus tracking.

Next time: Observe at peak hours for richer insights.

👀 Example B — College Cafeteria (Second Example)

  1. Students form long queues at only two billing counters during lunch hour.
  2. Several people leave the line after waiting more than 10 minutes.
  3. One counter accepts only cash; students with UPI search for another queue.
  4. Food trays run out of spoons; students walk back to request more.
  5. Seats near the window fill first; many stand and eat near the wall.

Surprised most by: How many students abandon the queue due to slow billing.

Hidden need: Faster payment options and more counters at peak time.

Next time: Observe both breakfast and lunch to compare crowd patterns.

🧪 Activity 1.2 Unit I

Conducting User Interviews

🎯 Objective: Practise open-ended interviewing 👤 Interviewee: College student commuter

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Write 7 open-ended questions (no yes/no questions).
  2. Find one real person (classmate, family, neighbour) who uses the service.
  3. Ask for permission before starting the interview.
  4. Interview for 15–30 minutes — listen more than you talk.
  5. Write exact words the person said (verbatim quotes).
  6. Note which pain point was repeated most often.

💬 Example A — Bus Commuter (Model Answer)

Interviewee: College student commuter

  1. What challenges do you face while waiting for buses?
  2. How do you usually check bus timings?
  3. What frustrates you most during commuting?
  4. Can you recall a recent bad commuting experience?
  5. What would make your bus travel easier?
  6. How do you feel about current bus stop facilities?
  7. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
“The timings on the app are never correct.”
“I wish there were more seats at the stop.”
“Sometimes I miss buses because announcements are unclear.”

Surprised by: Repeated complaints about inaccurate apps.

Pain point repeated: Lack of seating and unreliable information.

💬 Example B — Cafeteria User (Second Example)

Interviewee: Final-year student who eats at the college canteen daily

  1. What is the hardest part of getting lunch in the cafeteria?
  2. How do you usually decide what to order?
  3. What frustrates you most while waiting in the queue?
  4. Tell me about a recent bad experience in the canteen.
  5. What would make your lunch break easier?
  6. How do you feel about payment options available?
  7. If you could change one thing in the cafeteria, what would it be?
“By the time I reach the counter, half the items are over.”
“UPI works only on one counter — the queue is always longer there.”
“I often skip lunch because waiting wastes my free period.”

Surprised by: How many students skip meals due to long waits.

Pain point repeated: Slow billing and limited payment options at peak hour.

🧪 Activity 1.3 Unit I

Preparing an Empathy Map

👤 User: College student commuter

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Take your interview notes and observation facts.
  2. Draw 4 boxes on paper: SAYS · THINKS · DOES · FEELS.
  3. Put exact quotes under SAYS.
  4. Guess what the user was thinking under THINKS (from their words).
  5. Write actions you saw under DOES.
  6. Write emotions under FEELS.
  7. Find the deepest need from the map.

🗺 Example A — Bus Commuter Empathy Map

💬 SAYS “Bus timings are wrong.”   “No proper seating.”
🧠 THINKS “I might miss class if the bus is late.”
🏃 DOES Keeps checking phone, asks others for info.
❤️ FEELS Frustrated, anxious, tired.

“Says” vs “Thinks”: Outward complaints differ from inner anxiety.

Deepest need: Assurance of reliable bus arrival information.

🗺 Example B — Cafeteria User Empathy Map (Second Example)

💬 SAYS “The queue is too long.”   “Items get over before I reach.”
🧠 THINKS “If I wait more I will miss my next class.”
🏃 DOES Checks menu board, switches counters, sometimes leaves without food.
❤️ FEELS Impatient, hungry, stressed about time.

“Says” vs “Thinks”: Complains about queue length, but the real worry is missing class.

Deepest need: Faster service so lunch fits inside the free period.

🧪 Activity 1.4 Unit I

Developing a Persona

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Choose one main user type from your research (student, elderly, parent, worker…).
  2. Give them a realistic name, age, and job.
  3. Write their main goal (what they want to achieve).
  4. List their top 3 frustrations (from real quotes/observations).
  5. Write one signature quote in their own words.
  6. Do not invent details that contradict your research.

👤 Model Personas — 4 Categories (different ages)

Main model uses the student. Three more age groups are given so students can compare and adapt.

🎓 Ravi Kumar, 20 yrs

Category: College student

Role: Daily bus commuter

Goal: Reach college on time without stress

Top 3 Frustrations:

  1. Inaccurate bus timings
  2. No seating at bus stop
  3. Confusing announcements
“If buses came on time, my mornings would be peaceful.”
💼 Meena Sharma, 35 yrs

Category: Working professional

Role: Office employee, morning & evening commute

Goal: Reach office on time and return home safely

Top 3 Frustrations:

  1. Unpredictable delays affecting work
  2. Overcrowded buses in peak hours
  3. No clear real-time updates on phone
“I leave early every day, yet still reach late because of buses.”
👴 Subramaniam, 68 yrs

Category: Elderly citizen

Role: Retired, uses bus for hospital & market visits

Goal: Travel safely and comfortably without long standing

Top 3 Frustrations:

  1. No seats / long standing time
  2. Cannot read small text on digital boards
  3. Misses buses due to unclear announcements
“Standing for 20 minutes is very difficult at my age.”
👩‍👧 Priya, 42 yrs

Category: Parent with school-going child

Role: Mother escorting child to school by bus

Goal: Safe, on-time drop of child at school

Top 3 Frustrations:

  1. No safe waiting space for children
  2. Bus often late — child reaches school late
  3. Hard to manage child while checking timings
“I need the bus to be on time so my child is not late to school.”

🧠 Reflection — Model Answer

Most real: Frustrations matched actual interviews (especially student & elderly).

Design decision: Prioritize accurate bus tracking + seating + clearer announcements for all age groups.

🧪 Activity 1.5 Unit I

Preparing a User Journey Map

👤 Persona used: Ravi Kumar (main model)

👨‍🏫 What the Student Should Do

  1. Take one persona you created in Activity 1.4.
  2. List 5–6 stages of their day (from start to end of the experience).
  3. For each stage write: what they DO and how they FEEL.
  4. Mark the worst stage (biggest pain point) clearly.
  5. Write one opportunity statement (how design can help).
  6. Draw the stages in order (sequence diagram) left to right.

📚 Reference Unit I

Example Places for Observation & Interviews

1. Cafeteria / Canteen
Queueing, food choice, seating issues.
2. Library
Book search, catalog systems, silence rules.
3. Hospital waiting area
Patient anxiety, long waits, communication gaps.
4. ATM / Bank counter
Queues, machines, paperwork.
5. Supermarket checkout
Baskets, payment, bagging.
6. Public park
Facilities, litter, seating.
7. Railway / Metro stop
Ticketing, crowd flow, info boards.
8. Classroom / Lab
Technology, assignments, group work.
9. Government office (RTO)
Paperwork, waiting, confusion.
10. Mall escalators / lifts
Crowd movement, accessibility, safety.
📚 Reference Unit I

Example Themes for Empathy Maps & Personas

  1. Student struggling with online classes
  2. Parent managing small children in public spaces
  3. Elderly person using digital payment apps
  4. Tourist navigating a new city’s transport system
  5. Patient waiting for medical test results
  6. Customer trying to return a product in a store
  7. Job seeker filling out online applications

Why these work

  • Natural behaviour — people aren’t acting for the observer.
  • Visible frustrations — queues, confusion, waiting, discomfort.
  • Hidden needs — better information, comfort, accessibility.
  • Opportunities for innovation — apps, services, design changes.
